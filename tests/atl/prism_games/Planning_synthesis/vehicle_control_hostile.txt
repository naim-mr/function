Safe Vehicle Control in a Hostile Environment -- original (PRISM case study)
==========================================================================

Reference
---------
Tool : PRISM (MDP). https://www.prismmodelchecker.org/casestudies/
Model: probabilistically safe vehicle control in a hostile environment.

Informal model
--------------
A vehicle (e.g. a UAV/ground vehicle) must traverse a region toward a goal
while a hostile environment exposes it to threats. Threat occurrence is
stochastic; the vehicle chooses headings/lanes to minimise the probability of
being hit while still reaching the goal. The original PRISM analysis bounds
the probability of safe arrival.

De-probabilised version for FuncTion
------------------------------------
Keep the controllability split (vehicle = coalition, hostile environment =
adversary) and two qualitative objectives:

    << veh >>  G  ( never hit )        -- safety
    << veh >>  F  ( goal reached )     -- reachability

C transcription: ../Planning_synthesis/vehicle_control_hostile.c
  (each step the environment threatens a lane and the vehicle picks a lane;
   driving into the threatened lane is a hit; threats are bounded so the goal
   stays reachable.)

NOTE: the player/adversary split is natural here (the environment is
explicitly "hostile"). Drops the probabilistic threat model / safe-arrival
probability; keeps the strategic safety and reachability cores. Threats are
limited by an adversary-chosen finite budget (input("env"), not fixed in
advance -- see BOUNDED_ADVERSARY.md) so a purely blocking adversary cannot
stall the vehicle forever.

Why this modelling corresponds
------------------------------
Schematic abstraction at the game level:
  - controllability split: the vehicle is the coalition; the hostile threats
    are the uncontrolled (bounded) adversary;
  - objective classes: safety (never hit) and reachability (goal);
  - probabilistic threats -> bounded demonic non-determinism. So  << veh >> G
    !hit  and  << veh >> F goal  are the worst-case cores of the rPATL
    safe-arrival probability objective.
Preserved: vehicle-vs-threat structure + the safety and goal objectives.
Abstracted: the threat probabilities and the safe-arrival probability value.
