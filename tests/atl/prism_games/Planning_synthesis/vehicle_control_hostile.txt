Probabilistically Safe Vehicle Control in a Hostile Environment -- original
==========================================================================

Reference
---------
Tool : PRISM (MDP). https://www.prismmodelchecker.org/casestudies/
Paper: I. Cizelj, X. Chu Ding, M. Lahijanian, A. Pinto, C. Belta,
       "Probabilistically Safe Vehicle Control in a Hostile Environment",
       IFAC World Congress 2011 (arXiv:1103.4065).

Informal model (from the paper)
-------------------------------
A vehicle drives across a city partitioned into polytopic regions. One region
is the PICK-UP (rp), another the DROP-OFF (rd). The mission is: starting from an
initial region, reach rp to pick up a load, then reach rd to drop it off, while
never being LOST. The environment is threat-rich: each region holds a number of
moving adversaries (modelled as Poisson processes) and a static obstacle
density; crossing a region has a probability p_lost(n,o) of losing the vehicle
(mission failure). The whole thing is captured as an MDP whose only controller
is the vehicle (the adversary/obstacle dynamics are stochastic, not strategic).
The synthesised reactive strategy MAXIMISES the probability of the PCTL mission

    Pmax=? [ alive U ( rp & P>0 [ alive U rd ] ) ].

KEY POINT: the results are QUANTITATIVE and < 1 (Pmax = 0.141 and 0.805 for the
two adversary distributions of the case study). Even the best route keeps a
non-zero chance of being lost -- the mission is not a sure event.

De-probabilised version for FuncTion
------------------------------------
It is an MDP (a single controller), so we keep the vehicle as the coalition and
turn the stochastic loss into a demonic adversary: at each region crossing the
environment may inflict the residual loss the vehicle cannot rule out. We then
ask SURE completion of the two-stage mission while staying alive:

    << veh >>  F  ( delivered == 1 )

C transcription: ../Planning_synthesis/vehicle_control_hostile.c
  phase 0 -> heading to pick-up, phase 1 -> picked up / heading to drop-off,
  delivered when phase reaches 2 (drop-off) while alive. To progress the vehicle
  must cross (cross == 1); on a crossing the environment may set lost == 1,
  which loses the vehicle (alive = 0) and ends the mission unfulfilled. The
  "until" structure (rp before rd) is encoded by the phase counter; "staying
  alive" by the alive flag guarding the loop.

Expected result: UNKNOWN (soundness witness). To make progress the vehicle must
keep crossing threatened regions, and a worst-case environment can inflict the
residual loss on some crossing, so the vehicle cannot SURELY deliver. This is
exactly why the paper reports a maximal PROBABILITY < 1 rather than a sure
guarantee.

NOTE: earlier version modelled this as a lane-choice game with a bounded number
of avoidable threats, yielding TRUE for both a safety (<veh>G !hit) and a
reachability (<veh>F goal) objective. That was NOT faithful: the source is an
MDP whose optimal mission probability is < 1 (the loss is a residual,
unavoidable risk, not an avoidable threat), and the mission is the sequential
pick-up-then-drop-off objective above, not plain goal reachability. Abstracts:
the region partition, the adversary/obstacle distributions, and the numerical
loss probabilities p_lost(n,o).
