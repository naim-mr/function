Autonomous Urban Driving -- original model (PRISM-games case study)
=================================================================

Reference
---------
Tool : PRISM-games (stochastic two-player games, multi-objective synthesis).
Paper: T. Chen, M. Kwiatkowska, A. Simaitis, C. Wiltsche,
       "Synthesis for Multi-Objective Stochastic Games: An Application to
        Autonomous Urban Driving", QEST 2013 (DARPA Urban Challenge, real
        OpenStreetMap data).

Informal model (from the paper)
-------------------------------
A car drives a road network (a graph of road segments, imported from
OpenStreetMap) toward a destination. It is a stochastic two-player game:
Player 1 = the car (we synthesise its strategy), Player 2 = the environment.
On each segment the environment ADVERSARIALLY selects from a set of hazards
(pedestrian, jam, obstacle); the car picks a reaction (brake, honk, change
lane, U-turn). A reaction inappropriate for the road causes a rule violation;
an appropriate reaction still leads to an accident with a (small) PROBABILITY.
The specification is a multi-objective conjunctive query with a Pareto
trade-off: reach the target (F goal) with probability >= v1, AVOID accidents
(G not-accident) with probability >= v2, and keep expected road quality >= v3.
KEY POINT: the objectives TRADE OFF (Pareto) -- e.g. reaching faster incurs more
expected accidents/ROZ-like risk; accident probability is MINIMISED, not 0.

De-probabilised version for FuncTion
------------------------------------
Keep the controllability split (vehicle controller = coalition, hazards =
adversary) and two qualitative objectives:

    << car >>  G  ( no collision )         -- safety
    << car >>  F  ( destination reached )  -- reachability

C transcription: ../Planning_synthesis/autonomous_driving.c
  (the environment may present a hazard each round; advancing into a hazard
   collides, yielding is safe; hazards are bounded so the goal stays
   reachable.)

CAVEAT vs the paper: here the car can avoid EVERY collision by yielding, so
<car>G{crash==0} is TRUE. In the paper an appropriate reaction still causes an
accident with a small probability, so accident-avoidance is MINIMISED (Pareto),
not surely achieved -- a faithful qualitative variant would let the adversary
force a residual collision (making the safety property UNKNOWN, like the UAV ROZ
objective). We keep the simpler bounded-hazard abstraction here.

NOTE: drops the probabilistic hazard model and the route-cost reward of the
original rPATL specification; keeps the strategic safety and reachability
cores. Probabilistic hazards are modelled as an adversary-chosen finite budget
(input("env"), not fixed in advance -- see BOUNDED_ADVERSARY.md).

Why this modelling corresponds
------------------------------
Schematic abstraction at the game level (not a transcription of the source):
  - same controllability split: the vehicle controller is the coalition; the
    urban hazards are the uncontrolled environment;
  - same objective classes: safety (no collision) and reachability (arrival);
  - probabilistic hazards -> demonic non-determinism, BOUNDED so that a
    purely cancelling adversary cannot stall the car forever (mirrors the
    robot_coordination de-probabilisation). So  << car >> G !crash  and
    << car >> F arrived  are the qualitative cores of the rPATL safety /
    progress objectives.
Preserved: car-vs-hazard structure + the safety and arrival objectives.
Abstracted: the hazard probabilities and the route-cost reward.
