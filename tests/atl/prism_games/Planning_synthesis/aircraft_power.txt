Aircraft Power Distribution -- original model (PRISM-games case study)
====================================================================

Reference
---------
Tool : PRISM-games (turn-based stochastic games, long-run/ratio rewards).
Paper: N. Basset, M. Kwiatkowska, U. Topcu, C. Wiltsche,
       "Strategy Synthesis for Stochastic Games with Multiple Long-Run
        Objectives", TACAS 2015 (case study: aircraft primary electric power
        distribution; extends the game-theoretic model of Xu-Topcu-Murray).

Informal model (from the paper)
-------------------------------
An aircraft electrical power network routes power from generators to buses
(and their loads) through controllable contactors (switches). The contactors
are controlled by Player diamond (our coalition), while the contactor delays,
the interfaces, and the generator status (each generator powered w.p. pg,
refreshed every N steps) are controlled by Player box / stochastically. Short
circuits (e.g. connecting two generators) must be avoided. Because the system
is reactive (the aircraft runs continually), correctness is specified with
LONG-RUN RATIO rewards: keep the bus uptime (fraction of time the buses are
powered) above a threshold b, while keeping the failure rate below f -- a
multi-dimensional almost-sure satisfaction objective, synthesised
COMPOSITIONALLY (one sub-game per HVAC component) with fairness. The paper
obtains bus uptimes up to ~95% for generator health pg = 0.8.

De-probabilised version for FuncTion
------------------------------------
Keep the controllability split (control unit = coalition reconfiguring
contactors, generator outages = adversary) and a POWER-BALANCE safety
invariant (energy threshold), not a mere on/off flag:

    << ctrl >>  G  ( supplied power >= load demand )

C transcription: ../Planning_synthesis/aircraft_power.c
  (generators with a power capacity feed one critical bus with a power demand;
   each round the adversary takes one generator down and the control unit
   routes the bus to a generator. supplied = the connected generator's capacity
   (0 if it is the one down). The demand never exceeds a single generator's
   capacity and there are >= 2 generators, so a healthy source can always cover
   it. Capacity, demand and generator count are arbitrary, not fixed.)

NOTE: the original objective is a LONG-RUN RATIO reward (bus uptime fraction
>= b); we abstract it to a PER-ROUND power-adequacy invariant supplied >= demand
(if every round the load is met, the long-run uptime is 1). Drops the
probabilistic generator failure rates, the contactor delays, the compositional
structure and the quantitative uptime value; keeps the routing-vs-failure core.
Earlier versions used a bus on/off boolean with no energy quantities -- this
version models capacity vs demand explicitly, the faithful element of an
electrical-power case study.

Why this modelling corresponds
------------------------------
Schematic abstraction at the game level (not a transcription of the PRISM
source / AADL composition):
  - same controllability split: the bus control unit is the coalition; the
    generator failures are the uncontrolled environment;
  - same objective class: a SAFETY invariant "the critical load is met"
    (supplied power >= demand);
  - probabilistic generator failures -> demonic non-determinism (the
    adversary takes one generator down each round). So  << ctrl >> G (supplied
    >= demand)  is the qualitative core of  P_max[ G powered ] = 1 / the
    expected-uptime objective, i.e. "can the control unit keep the load met
    whatever fails".
Preserved: control-vs-failure structure + the power-adequacy invariant.
Abstracted: the failure probabilities and the expected-uptime reward.
